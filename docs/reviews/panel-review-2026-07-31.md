# Quant-Engine Review Panel — CTO Synthesis & Roadmap

_Date: 2026-07-31 · Method: 6 IC reviewers (2 staff SWE, 2 senior AI eng, 2 senior quant dev) → 3 adversarial red-team verifiers → CTO synthesis. 18 findings raised, 17 survived verification (1 refuted, 6 downgraded). Every claim is traceable to `file:line`._

## 1. Executive summary

This is a genuinely strong, safety-conscious codebase. The verification discipline itself is a good sign: of the panel's findings, the red team **refuted P6.1** outright (the friction/capacity "different denominator" claim — both are per-bar participation ratios, so the 24x/288x resolution artifact does not exist) and **downgraded five findings** (P1.2, P3.2, P4.1, P4.2, P4.3, P6.2) once the impact claims failed to survive the actual gating logic. What remains is credible and mostly narrow. The three things that matter most: **(1) statistical honesty of the promotion evidence** — the CPCV "out-of-sample" distribution is computed on in-sample returns (P4.1), its purge/embargo is decorative (P4.3), and the seal records none of the pass/fail verdict it was judged against (P4.4); **(2) the shipped ensemble objective is effectively a pure decorrelation search** because a unit-scale correlation penalty swamps per-bar return/CVaR terms ~1000x smaller (P3.1); and **(3) two fail-closed gaps at irreversible decision points** — the production seal trusts the audit log without verifying its HMAC chain (P2.1), and `Config::load` boots on hard-coded defaults when the config file is missing (P1.1). None of these is a data-corruption or money-ledger bug — the fixed-point money math, look-ahead controls, and determinism harness are excellent — but for a quant engine that *markets* reproducibility and overfitting honesty, the credibility surface is where the real risk lives.

## 2. What's genuinely strong (do not refactor away)

- **Money math and look-ahead safety are exemplary.** `domain/money.rs` re-runs validating constructors inside `Deserialize` (serde can't smuggle a negative price/qty), notional has a single named rounding point with proptests; feature catalogue is causal ring buffers with proven batch==streaming parity; historical decoder drops the forming right-edge bar and in-progress funding against an injected `now_ms`.
- **Determinism-by-construction is proven, not asserted.** `task_rng(master, index)` + rayon ordered `collect` gives byte-identical archives at 1 vs 8 threads (`permuted_parallelism.rs`), with a non-vacuous guard showing a shared RNG *would* break it. Content-hashed types are Vec/scalar-only; no HashMap iteration-order nondeterminism reaches output.
- **The overfitting statistics are correctly implemented.** PSR/DSR are exactly the Bailey/López de Prado estimators (correct variance term, non-excess kurtosis, `sqrt(T-1)` scaling); the large-N deflation-bar fix avoids the `1-1/N` catastrophic cancellation; SPA is honestly labelled as the conservative White RC variant. Empty/degenerate CPCV and robustness inputs **fail closed**.
- **Governance and auth are fail-closed where it counts.** Session verify is constant-time; off-loopback ephemeral-secret boot is refused; RBAC is resolved server-side per request, never from the cookie. The seal *predicate* is a pure, unit-tested fail-closed function and the self-approve/unresolved-launcher hole is explicitly closed.
- **The firewall is a real executable invariant** (transitive reachability, tests assert known real edges were actually parsed so a parser bug can't pass vacuously), and QE-411's blocking-fs discipline is enforced with a guard test — which is exactly why the two gaps below (P1.2, P2.2) are notable as *exceptions* to an otherwise-held line.

## 4. Quick wins (this week)

All are `quick-win` effort with outsized value:

1. **P2.1** — one `verify_chain` call + 409 at `production_seal`; the chain is already read. Closes a fail-open at the single irreversible governance action.
2. **P1.1** — flip figment to `required(true)` / add an `.exists()` guard. One line moves a live-run boot from fail-open to fail-closed.
3. **P4.2** — bump `CSCV_BLOCKS` 2→8–10, keep the thin-run fallback, re-baseline goldens.
4. **P4.4 (partial)** — even before sealing the full verdict, gate the write on `g1.promoted` so a failed vintage cannot be persisted as promotable. The full "seal the decision + thresholds" is the moderate follow-up.
5. **P3.3** and **P6.3** — small correctness/durability fixes (truncate before `pearson`; single-txn provenance write) that remove latent traps cheaply.

## 5. Strategic / refactor themes

**Theme A — Make the sealed vintage self-describing and self-validating (P4.4 + P4.1 + P4.3 + P1.3 + P5.1).**
Today the seal is a content-addressed bag of *raw figures* whose trustworthiness lives entirely in ephemeral train-job state and drift-prone constants. The strategic fix is to make the artifact carry its own verdict, thresholds, and reproducibility guarantees. *Trade-off:* sealing the `G1Decision` and enforcing rounding at the type boundary is cheap and high-leverage; feeding CPCV genuine OOS via **per-split refit** is expensive (refit cost × combinatorial paths) and is the only thing that makes purge/embargo (P4.3) load-bearing. *Sequence:* (1) seal the verdict+thresholds and gate the write (P4.4) — biggest credibility win, lowest cost; (2) move rounding into `seal()` (P1.3) and get regime labels off f64 or out of the hash (P5.1); (3) *then* decide CPCV: either refit-per-split (do it right) or feed the frozen holdout and stop branding a fixed-candidate distribution as "purged, leak-free OOS." Do not ship (3) as-is — pick one honest story.

**Theme B — Statistical-honesty calibration of the selection objective and gate resolution (P3.1 + P4.1 + P4.2).**
The engine's headline is overfitting honesty, yet the *selection* objective is mis-scaled (P3.1) and the *primary* gate is a 3-value estimator (P4.2). *Debate:* for P3.1, normalizing terms to per-unit-risk is more robust than hand-tuning `corr_weight`, but it changes every historical selection and forces a golden re-baseline and possibly a re-seal — so it needs explicit quant-owner sign-off on intended behaviour (§6). *Sequence first:* raise CSCV blocks (P4.2, isolated, cheap) before touching the objective, so you have a sharper gate to evaluate the objective change against.

**Theme C — Fail-closed everywhere an irreversible or boot decision is made (P2.1 + P1.1).**
Two independent fail-open gaps at the two moments that matter most (seal, boot). Cheap individually; worth treating as a *posture audit* — grep every `read_all`/config-load/launcher-resolution site for a "trusts unauthenticated/absent input" pattern and assert the fail-closed default. Low cost, directly protects the governance and reproducibility claims the codebase is built on.

**Theme D — Extend QE-411 async-I/O discipline to the supervisor path (P2.2 + P2.3).**
The blocking-fs and serialized-RMW disciplines are enforced *everywhere except* the run supervisor. This is a consistency/latency investment, not a correctness emergency. Sequence after A–C; bundle both into one pass over `runs/manager.rs` and extend the existing guard test to cover the supervisor.

## 6. Dissent & open questions

- **P4.1 / P4.2 / P4.3 — the red team's downgrades are correct, and I want the panel on record about it.** The original "critical/high" framing claimed the CPCV/PBO issues *break* the OOS guarantee. They do not: `evaluate_g1` still runs on the genuine `holdout_bars` and is conjoined (AND) with the additive CPCV gate; PBO's `pbo==0.0` requirement is strict, not lax. These are **honesty/branding defects on the evidence surface**, not gating holes. That distinction changes the fix (relabel/refit vs. patch a broken gate) and the urgency (P1, not P0).
- **P4.4 severity vs. priority — I am overriding rank above the adjusted severity.** The red team rated it medium (correctly, on narrow blast radius). I rank it **P0** because "a sealed artifact cannot prove it passed its own gate, and re-classifies when a constant drifts" strikes at the engine's central promise. This is a priority judgment, not a challenge to the verification. **Human decision needed:** should a G1-failed candidate be *un-writable*, or written-but-marked? The former is safer; the latter preserves negative-result lineage. Pick before implementing.
- **P3.1 — needs product/quant-owner intent before recalibration.** The finding proves the objective is decorrelation-dominated under shipped defaults; it does *not* prove that's unintended. If decorrelation-first is the deliberate house view, the fix is to *document* it and fix the misleading docstring, not to reweight. Either way this touches sealed history — do not silently change the default.
- **CPCV strategic fork (Theme A/B).** Per-split refit is the statistically right answer but materially more expensive. Feeding the frozen holdout is cheap and honest but sacrifices the combinatorial-path value CPCV was added for. This is a cost/value call for the quant lead, not an engineering default.
- **P6.2 lot/tick/minNotional — ADR, not a bug.** The complete absence is confirmed; the P&L impact for the *deployed liquid-major universe* is not established. Decide explicitly: model exchange filters, or ADR them out of scope and gate thin-name deployment on the existing liquidity screen.
- **P1.2 — accept-with-guard vs. accept-as-is.** Zero live instances, deliberate-bypass-only, and the idiomatic `.workspace=true` path is already caught. A cheap rename-evasion unit test closes the latent gap; a full target-resolution rewrite is not worth it now.
- **Verification quality note.** I found no finding the red team appears to have mis-*verified*. P6.1's refutation (both participation denominators are per-bar; base-vs-dollar cancels) is sound and correctly discarded. My only departures from their output are *priority* calls (P4.4 elevated), explicitly justified above — I have used their adjusted severities as the severity input throughout.
